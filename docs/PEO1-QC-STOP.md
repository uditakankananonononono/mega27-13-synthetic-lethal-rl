# PEO1 source QC stop before any ranker result
GSE123290 raw workbook https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE123nnn/GSE123290/suppl/ , SHA256 6aab064fe3d66ce76848b9f547876498b84d8aa852f0e814b8a354e75390a26d. Official study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710300/ prioritizes A10 vs S10. Descriptive QC script/results are `scripts/peo1_qc.py` and `results/peo1-guide-qc.json`; no candidate scoring or model benchmark has run.

119,461 guide rows; strict single-symbol parser retains 111,314, excluding 8,147 multi-gene/ambiguous identifiers (many `#`-separated targets; not silently assigned to first gene). Sample totals range about 24.3M-32.9M counts, but sample medians vary sharply despite similar totals: A10_A/A10_B ~169/173, A10_C 2; S10_A 121, S10_B 33; Ctrl_A/B 3/6, Ctrl_C 183. For single-symbol guides passing Ctrl median >=20 and >=2 guides/gene, raw log2-count Pearson correlations are A10_A-B 0.311, A10_A-C 0.246, A10_B-C 0.244, S10_A-B 0.299. These are lower than expected for a clean replicable guide library and may reflect sample/library composition, indexing/schema errors or protocol properties. They DO NOT establish a causal assay defect without reconciliation. The low-baseline filter itself can induce misleading Ctrl anticorrelation; do not interpret filtered Ctrl correlations as library quality.

STOP: do not benchmark a source-transferred model or call an anoikis vulnerability until sample identity, guide library A/B mapping, depth-normalized replicate QC, day-10 design and within-gene concordance are reconciled to the paper or an independent processor. A10/S10 not interchangeable with day-5. The original locked synthetic-lethality gate remains separate and failed. This exploratory pivot can be abandoned without inventing a result.

Partition investigation (script `scripts/peo1_library_audit.py`, result `results/peo1-library-partition-qc.json`): GeneID prefixes divide 63,762 `HGLibA` and 55,448 `HGLibB` rows plus 251 other. Addgene's source https://www.addgene.org/pooled-library/zhang-human-gecko-v2/ confirms GeCKO v2 A and B are two distinct half-libraries with ~3 guides/gene each. They cannot be presumed equally represented in every sample. Indeed `HGLibA` median counts are near zero in A5 triplicates and S10 duplicates while `HGLibB` medians are high there; `HGLibB` is near zero in Ctrl_A and A10_C, while `HGLibA` remains high in Ctrl_C. FOLLOW-UP CORRECTION: although medians vary sharply, aggregate HGLibA fraction of A+B counts is 42.2%-42.8% in ALL 14 samples, so a simple unequal TOTAL half-library mixture explanation is not supported. Distributional shifts, control composition, indexing or protocol effects remain unresolved; median differences must not be taken as proof of sample mislabeling. Naive pooling without guide-level QC remains unsafe. No source-faithful day-10 comparison until library-specific compatible subsets and paper metadata are reconciled. These QC findings are methodological cautions, not a cancer discovery.

Additional descriptive sample-similarity check (`scripts/peo1_similarity_audit.py`, `results/peo1-sample-similarity-audit.json`, source same workbook): across all 119,461 guide rows, unfiltered log2(count+1) Pearson correlations for declared A10_A/B/C are 0.394/0.314/0.315 pairwise, and S10_A/B is 0.373. Partition-wise A10_A/B correlations are 0.344 (HGLibA) and 0.416 (HGLibB), S10_A/B 0.335 and 0.377. Some non-replicate sample pairs rank more similar than declared replicates, also in each half-library. This is only a diagnostic, not proof of sample mislabeling or failed biological replicates: systematic guide-count distribution, screen design and count processing could drive it. The study explicitly states that its main A10-vs-S10 comparison used duplicates after PCA and clustering (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710300/ , Results, CRISPR/Cas9 screen), so our naive triplicate analysis must not claim to reproduce the published test. STOP remains: reconcile raw workbook with the actual paper inclusion and normalization before a ranker or new finding.
